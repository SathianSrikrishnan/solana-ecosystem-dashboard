## Working thesis

I was interested in Solana as a future financial rail, but I was still running
on other people's summaries. Building the observatory forced me to define what
"healthy" means, follow the numbers to their sources, and trace today's
stablecoins, tokenized assets, trading, payments, and validators back through
seven years of invention, failure, recovery, and expansion.

The two worlds connected are **a personal apprenticeship in Solana** and **the
construction of new financial infrastructure**.

## Opening scene

Begin at the dashboard, not at Solana's founding. Sathian is staring at a page
that confidently says millions of addresses used Solana while realizing he
cannot yet explain how many are people, bots, signers, fee payers, or customers.
That discomfort becomes the reason to build the observatory and follow the
evidence backward.

The opening should use a real moment from the build: the first Dune query, the
first live dashboard view, or explaining the project to his children. The final
choice needs one specific memory from Sathian rather than an invented scene.

## Narrative structure

### 1. The instrument panel

Personal problem: wanting conviction without pretending expertise. Introduce
the six questions—network, adoption, economy, validators, ecosystem, and
financial rails—without turning the opening into a product demo.

### 2. 2017–2020 — The clock before consensus

Proof of History is conceived in 2017; Mainnet Beta launches March 16, 2020.
Explain the original bet simply: a fast shared clock could let a distributed
network coordinate much more efficiently. Distinguish a technical thesis from
a proven economy.

### 3. 2021–2022 — The boom finds its limits

DeFi and NFTs bring capital and applications. Then outages make reliability
impossible to treat as a footnote. Include the FTX/Alameda shock only after a
separate independent-source pass; first-party retrospectives are incomplete.

### 4. 2023 — What survives

Community recovery, renewed DeFi, Saga, Helium, state compression, and builders
who kept shipping. The point is not that price recovered; it is that people
continued building after the story became unfashionable.

### 5. 2024–2025 — From speed to reliability and economics

Payments, stablecoins, mobile, DePIN, institutional experiments, longer uptime,
application revenue, and multiple validator-client efforts broaden the thesis.
Balance the success with validator concentration and software-diversity risk.

### 6. 2026 — The rails become visible

Stablecoin settlement, tokenized equities/funds/commodities, payments
infrastructure, and deeper crypto-native markets converge. Carefully separate
announcements from deployed assets, value from liquidity, and stablecoin
transfers from actual payments.

### 7. The dashboard is the argument

Return to the observatory. The conclusion is not that Solana has already won.
It is that the correct way to participate is to build instruments that can
prove—or challenge—the thesis as the system changes.

## Evidence rules

- Exact dates and technical history use official primary sources.
- Foundation growth figures are labeled as Foundation-reported.
- Live claims use reproducible RPC, Dune, DeFiLlama, or approved adapters.
- The 2022 FTX/Alameda section requires explicit authorization for strong
  independent reporting because first-party sources cannot tell it fully.
- Wallets are not people; TVL is not adoption; volume is not revenue; REV is
  not profit; stablecoin transfers are not automatically payments.

long-form explanation. The dashboard remains current and deterministic; the
