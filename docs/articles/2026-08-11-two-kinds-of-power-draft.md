# Two Kinds of Power

Status: first review draft

Author: Sathian Srikrishnan

Target: Crypto 101 companion page / later sathian.ai article

Domains: family + programmable money

## Draft

I have twin daughters, so I notice pairs.

In the Hindu stories and symbols I grew up around, Saraswati represents
knowledge, learning, speech, and music. Lakshmi represents prosperity,
abundance, and good fortune. They are not a neat binary, and Hindu traditions
do not all read them the same way. But I return to them as a personal shorthand:
learn deeply, then use value wisely.

That balance feels newly urgent.

Artificial intelligence is making knowledge cheaper to produce and easier to
act on. Crypto is trying to make ownership and settlement programmable. One
system can decide. The other can keep the receipt.

Bitcoin’s 2008 whitepaper described digital money that strangers could verify
without one central bookkeeper. Ethereum made that shared ledger programmable.
Solana pushed the idea toward fast, inexpensive execution—closer to the speed
and cost ordinary products need.

That does not make Solana magic. Its performance comes with real trade-offs:
demanding validator hardware, concentrated stake, and an operational history
worth inspecting. Crypto also earned some of its reputation for scams. A cheap,
open rail gives builders room to create; it gives bandits room to operate too.

I am learning by building instead of pretending I already know the answer. The
Solana Observatory tests whether the network is reliable, used, economically
productive, and becoming real financial infrastructure. Tooth Fairy Network
tests a smaller, more personal question: can programmable money help families
preserve memories and create longer-term incentives for children?

Saraswati and Lakshmi are not technology predictions. They are a reminder that
intelligence without judgment can become noise, and prosperity without wisdom
can become extraction.

The future will not be shaped by intelligence alone or money alone. It will be
shaped by what we know, what we value, and what the ledger can prove.
