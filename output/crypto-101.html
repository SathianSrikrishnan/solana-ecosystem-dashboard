Two Kinds
of Power

What Saraswati, Lakshmi, public ledgers, and two daughters taught me about programmable money.

I have twin daughters, so I notice pairs.

In the Hindu stories and symbols I grew up around, Saraswati represents knowledge, learning, speech, and music. Lakshmi represents prosperity, abundance, and good fortune. They are not a neat binary, and Hindu traditions do not all read them the same way. But I return to them as a personal shorthand: learn deeply, then use value wisely.

That balance feels newly urgent.

Artificial intelligence is making knowledge cheaper to produce and easier to act on. Crypto is trying to make ownership and settlement programmable. One system can decide. The other can keep the receipt.

Bitcoin’s 2008 whitepaper described digital money that strangers could verify without one central bookkeeper. Ethereum made that shared ledger programmable. Solana pushed the idea toward fast, inexpensive execution—closer to the speed and cost ordinary products need.

That does not make Solana magic. Its performance comes with real trade-offs: demanding validator hardware, concentrated stake, and an operational history worth inspecting. Crypto also earned some of its reputation for scams. A cheap, open rail gives builders room to create; it gives bandits room to operate too.

I am learning by building instead of pretending I already know the answer. The Solana Observatory tests whether the network is reliable, used, economically productive, and becoming real financial infrastructure. Tooth Fairy Network tests a smaller, more personal question: can programmable money help families preserve memories and create longer-term incentives for children?

Saraswati and Lakshmi are not technology predictions. They are a reminder that intelligence without judgment can become noise, and prosperity without wisdom can become extraction.

The future will be shaped by what we know, what we value, and what the ledger can prove.
Why a ledger? A ledger is simply a record of who owns what and what happened. A public blockchain makes that record independently checkable. It does not make every entry wise, fair, or true about the outside world.

Four technical turns

From digital money to agentic rails.

2008 / Bitcoin

Truth without one bookkeeper

A public ledger made digital scarcity and peer-to-peer settlement work without a central payment operator.

2015 / Ethereum

The ledger becomes programmable

General-purpose smart contracts let applications share rules, assets, and settlement onchain.

2020 / Solana

Faster, cheaper execution

Solana redesigned a layer one around verifiable ordering, parallel execution, and low-cost activity.

Next / Agentic rails

Software can act—and settle

Agents can make decisions. Open rails can give those decisions transparent rules, ownership, and receipts.
